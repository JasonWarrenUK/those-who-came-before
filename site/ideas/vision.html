Start here · The premise

Nobody Wrote the Answer Down

There is a true history behind this game. Every culture, every trade route, every reason an object was made the way it was: all of it is fixed, complete and consistent. And nothing in the game will ever confirm any part of it to you. There is no solution screen. There was never going to be one.

Not even the scholars know

That might sound like an ordinary mystery-game promise: figure it out yourself, we won't hold your hand. It isn't that. In most games with a hidden truth, someone in the fiction knows it. A wise elder, a diary, a final cutscene. Ask enough questions and the world tells you.

Here, nobody in the fiction knows it either. The other scholars, the ones whose papers you read before you've excavated a single site, are working from the same permanently partial view you are. Their published claims aren't answers held back from you; they're guesses, some better supported than others, made by people with the same access to the past that you have: none, directly, ever.

Nobody wrote it down

A hand-authored mystery has an author who knows the answer, which means the answer exists somewhere, in someone's head or someone's notes, and can in principle be found, argued about, and settled. Ours doesn't work that way. The true history of a given seed is a consequence of running that seed, not a fact anyone decided and typed in. Nobody has read it end to end. Not the person who built the generator, either: they'd have to run the seed and look at the output, the same as anyone would.

That's a genuinely different thing to promise a player than "we haven't told you yet".

Thousands of them

Change the seed string and you get a different world: different cultures, different trade networks, a different true answer to every question you might ask. One seed is one entire discipline's worth of unrecoverable past. Nothing about any single one of them is precious on its own; what's being built is a machine for producing them, not one particular history to protect.

Which means the game was never really about finding out what happened in this world. It's about what it's like to practise the discipline of trying to find out, when the honest answer is that you mostly can't, and the people around you can't either, and everyone is going to publish something anyway.

What refusing an author costs

No hand-placed twist. No moment where a designer, hiding behind the world, decides to surprise you on purpose. No character who exists to tell you the truth once you've earned it. If something interesting happens, it emerged from cultures, geology and generations of simulated scholarship colliding, or it didn't happen at all.

That's a real constraint, and it's worth saying plainly what it costs: it means the game can't guarantee a satisfying ending, because nobody designed one. It can only guarantee that whatever you find, nobody arranged it for you to find. This is a specification and a partly-built engine, documented here as it's built. Nobody has played the finished thing yet, including the people building it.
