Engineering Notes

How an Artefact Gets Made, and Why the Design Argues Back

Those Who Came Before is a game about an archaeologist whose mistakes compound into an unreliable narrative. These pages document three things: the ideas underneath it, in plain language; the design that turns misreading an artefact into the point; and the generation pipeline that makes every artefact a real object with a structure, materials and a ground truth the player never reads directly.

No prior archaeology required. No prior game development either, and no prior software background: the Ideas pages need neither.

Why the past has no author, and why one seed is never enough. 02 The Discipline That Already Happened A Whole Career, Simulated Decades of scholarship exist before you start, built by simulating dead researchers' careers. Roughly seven claims in ten hold up. The other three are wrong for reasons real archaeology recognises. 03 How You Get Fooled Selected, Not Invented Two readers, one true object, two confident and opposite conclusions. Nothing shown to either of them was false. 04 Time You Have to Earn Nobody Shares a Calendar Every culture keeps its own periods, and nothing tells you where they overlap. Order is free. A date costs you time, standing, and sometimes part of the object. 05 Culture Over Time Rich Compared to When? Independent attributes rather than a single score, shifting across phases nobody announces. What counts as lavish is judged against what came just before, never the culture's own average.

Nothing on this strand carries a shipped/partial/designed badge. Those badges answer "does it run", which is the wrong question for a page whose subject is a decision, not a component. Each page says plainly in prose what's a shipped type a reader can open and what's still a doc's intention with no type behind it.

A document classifies itself from its own content, then a venue classifies it again.
